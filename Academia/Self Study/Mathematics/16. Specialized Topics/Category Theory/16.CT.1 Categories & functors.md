## Definition

A category consists of objects, morphisms between objects, identity morphisms, and associative composition. A functor $F:C\to D$ maps objects and morphisms while preserving identities and composition: $F(g\circ f)=F(g)\circ F(f)$.

## Examples

Sets with functions, groups with homomorphisms, and topological spaces with continuous maps are categories. The forgetful functor from groups to sets keeps the underlying set and function.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify the category axioms for sets and functions.
2. Give a functor from vector spaces to sets.
3. Explain why a functor preserves isomorphisms.
