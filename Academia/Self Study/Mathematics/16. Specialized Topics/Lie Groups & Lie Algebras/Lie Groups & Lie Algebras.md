## Overview

Lie theory connects smooth continuous symmetry with algebraic infinitesimal data.

## Key Concepts

A Lie group is both a smooth manifold and a group with smooth multiplication and inversion. Its Lie algebra is the tangent space at the identity with bracket given by the commutator of left-invariant vector fields. The exponential map sends algebra elements to one-parameter subgroups.

## Major Results

The Lie correspondence turns homomorphisms into bracket-preserving linear maps. The Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula describes local group multiplication. Representations encode group symmetries as linear transformations.

## Practice Problems

1. Compute the Lie algebra of $GL_n(\mathbb{R})$.
2. Show that an abelian Lie group has an abelian Lie algebra.
3. Find the exponential of a diagonal $2\times2$ matrix.
4. Explain why one-parameter subgroups are determined by tangent vectors at the identity.
