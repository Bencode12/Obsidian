## Overview

Partial differential equations describe functions of several variables through their partial derivatives. They model diffusion, waves, transport, fluids, and fields.

## Key Concepts

The heat equation $u_t=\kappa\Delta u$ is parabolic, the wave equation $u_{tt}=c^2\Delta u$ is hyperbolic, and Laplace's equation $\Delta u=0$ is elliptic. Boundary and initial conditions select a well-posed problem. Weak solutions permit derivatives to be interpreted through integration by parts.

## Major Results

The maximum principle controls extrema of many elliptic and parabolic equations. Energy estimates establish uniqueness and stability. Fourier series, Green's functions, separation of variables, and the method of characteristics provide core solution techniques.

## Practice Problems

1. Classify the heat, wave, and Laplace equations.
2. Solve the one-dimensional heat equation using separation of variables for homogeneous boundary data.
3. Derive the characteristic curves for $u_t+cu_x=0$.
4. Explain the difference between a classical and a weak solution.
