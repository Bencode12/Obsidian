## Definition

A map $f : X \to Y$ between topological spaces is an **open map** if $f(U)$ is open in $Y$ whenever $U$ is open in $X$.

---

## Open Mapping Theorem (Banach-Schauder)

> [!example] Open Mapping Theorem
> Let $X$ and $Y$ be **Banach spaces** (complete normed spaces).
> If $T : X \to Y$ is a **bounded linear operator** that is **surjective** (onto), then $T$ is an **open map**.

---

## Corollaries

The Open Mapping Theorem yields two foundational pillars of functional analysis:

### Bounded Inverse Theorem

> [!example] Bounded Inverse Theorem
> Let $X$ and $Y$ be Banach spaces. If $T : X \to Y$ is a continuous linear **bijection** (one-to-one and onto), then its inverse $T^{-1} : Y \to X$ is automatically **bounded** (continuous)!

### Closed Graph Theorem

Let $T : X \to Y$ be a linear operator between Banach spaces. The **graph** of $T$ is:

$$\Gamma(T) = \{(x, Tx) \mid x \in X\} \subseteq X \times Y$$

> [!example] Closed Graph Theorem
> $T$ is bounded (continuous) if and only if its graph $\Gamma(T)$ is a **closed subset** of $X \times Y$.

---

## Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Completeness of Both Spaces is Mandatory!
> The Open Mapping Theorem fails if either $X$ or $Y$ is incomplete (not a Banach space). Always verify that both domain and codomain are Banach spaces before applying!

## Practice Problems

1. State the Open Mapping Theorem and list all required hypotheses.
2. Prove the Bounded Inverse Theorem as a corollary of the Open Mapping Theorem.
3. Show by counterexample that the Bounded Inverse Theorem fails if the domain space $X$ is not complete.
4. State the Closed Graph Theorem and explain how it simplifies proving operator continuity.
5. Let $X, Y$ be Banach spaces and let $T : X \to Y$ be a continuous bijective linear operator. Show that there exist constants $c, C > 0$ such that $c \|x\| \le \|Tx\| \le C \|x\|$ for all $x \in X$.
