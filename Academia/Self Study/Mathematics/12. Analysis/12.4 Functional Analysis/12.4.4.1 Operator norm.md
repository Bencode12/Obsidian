## Definition

Let $X$ and $Y$ be normed vector spaces over $\mathbb{K}$ ($\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$).

A linear map $T : X \to Y$ is called **bounded** if there exists a real constant $M \geq 0$ such that:

$$\|T x\|_Y \leq M \|x\|_X \quad \forall x \in X$$

The **operator norm** of $T$, denoted $\|T\|$, is defined as the smallest such bound:

$$\|T\| = \sup_{x \neq 0} \frac{\|T x\|_Y}{\|x\|_X} = \sup_{\|x\|_X \le 1} \|T x\|_Y = \sup_{\|x\|_X = 1} \|T x\|_Y$$

---

## Boundedness vs Continuity

> [!example] Fundamental Equivalence Theorem
> Let $T : X \to Y$ be a linear operator between normed spaces. The following are equivalent:
> 1. $T$ is **bounded** ($\|T\| < \infty$).
> 2. $T$ is **continuous** on all of $X$.
> 3. $T$ is **continuous at the origin** $0 \in X$.
> 4. $T$ is **Lipschitz continuous** on $X$ with Lipschitz constant $\|T\|$.

---

## Space of Bounded Linear Operators $\mathcal{B}(X, Y)$

The space of all bounded linear operators from $X$ to $Y$, denoted $\mathcal{B}(X, Y)$ (or $L(X, Y)$), forms a normed vector space under operator addition, scalar multiplication, and operator norm $\|T\|$.

> [!info] Banach Space Property of $\mathcal{B}(X, Y)$
> If $Y$ is a **Banach space** (complete normed space), then $\mathcal{B}(X, Y)$ is also a **Banach space**, regardless of whether $X$ is complete!

---

## Submultiplicativity

If $T \in \mathcal{B}(X, Y)$ and $S \in \mathcal{B}(Y, Z)$, then the composition $S \circ T \in \mathcal{B}(X, Z)$ satisfies:

$$\|S \circ T\| \leq \|S\| \cdot \|T\|$$

This makes $\mathcal{B}(X) = \mathcal{B}(X, X)$ a **normed algebra** (and a **Banach algebra** if $X$ is complete).

## Practice Problems

1. Show that the four definitions of operator norm $\|T\| = \sup_{x \neq 0} \frac{\|Tx\|}{\|x\|} = \sup_{\|x\| \le 1} \|Tx\| = \sup_{\|x\| = 1} \|Tx\|$ are all equivalent.
2. Prove that a linear operator $T$ is continuous if and only if it is continuous at $0$.
3. Compute the operator norm of the diagonal operator $T : \ell^2 \to \ell^2$ given by $T(x_1, x_2, \dots) = (\lambda_1 x_1, \lambda_2 x_2, \dots)$ where $(\lambda_n)$ is a bounded sequence.
4. Prove submultiplicativity: $\|S \circ T\| \le \|S\| \cdot \|T\|$.
5. Give an example of an unbounded linear operator on an infinite-dimensional normed space (e.g. differentiation operator on polynomials).
