## Overview

Analysis makes calculus precise and studies limits, continuity, differentiation, integration, and infinite-dimensional spaces. The central theme is controlling approximation with definitions that support proofs.

| Branch | Main questions |
|---|---|
| [[12.1 Real Analysis I]] | How do sequences, series, and continuous functions behave? |

| [[12.3 Measure Theory]] | How can size and integration be defined beyond intervals? |
| [[12.4 Functional Analysis]] | How do analysis and linear algebra interact in normed spaces? |

## Prerequisites

> [!info] Prerequisites
> Be comfortable with proof by contradiction, supremum and infimum arguments, sequences, basic set theory, linear algebra, and single-variable calculus.

---

## Core Ideas

- Completeness turns Cauchy information into actual limits.
- Continuity preserves limiting behavior.
- Differentiation is local linear approximation.
- Integration aggregates local information and requires convergence control.
- Norms and metrics provide a language for convergence in general spaces.

---

## Practice Problems

1. Prove that a convergent sequence in a metric space is Cauchy.
2. Give an example of pointwise convergence that is not uniform.
3. Explain why differentiability at a point implies continuity there.
4. State a condition under which an interchange of limit and integral is valid.
5. Compare completeness of a normed space with compactness of a subset.
