## Closed Sets

Let $(X, \mathcal{T})$ be a topological space. A subset $C \subseteq X$ is **closed** if its complement $X \setminus C$ is open ($X \setminus C \in \mathcal{T}$).

| Property | Open Sets ($\mathcal{T}$) | Closed Sets |
|---|---|---|
| **Base elements** | $\varnothing, X$ are open | $\varnothing, X$ are closed |
| **Unions** | Arbitrary unions are open | Finite unions are closed |
| **Intersections** | Finite intersections are open | Arbitrary intersections are closed |

> [!warning] "Open" and "Closed" are Not Antonyms!
> A subset can be:
> - **Both open and closed** ("clopen"): e.g., $\varnothing$ and $X$ in any space; or $(-\infty, 0)$ in $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$.
> - **Neither open nor closed**: e.g., $[0, 1)$ in $\mathbb{R}$ with the standard topology.

## Interior, Closure, and Boundary

Let $A \subseteq X$.

### Interior ($\text{int}(A)$ or $A^\circ$)

The **interior** of $A$ is the largest open set contained within $A$:

$$\text{int}(A) = \bigcup \{U \subseteq X \mid U \text{ is open and } U \subseteq A\}$$

### Closure ($\text{cl}(A)$ or $\bar{A}$)

The **closure** of $A$ is the smallest closed set containing $A$:

$$\text{cl}(A) = \bigcap \{C \subseteq X \mid C \text{ is closed and } A \subseteq C\}$$

### Boundary ($\partial A$)

The **boundary** (or frontier) of $A$ is the set of points in the closure of $A$ but not in its interior:

$$\partial A = \bar{A} \setminus \text{int}(A) = \bar{A} \cap \overline{X \setminus A}$$

---

## Limit Points and Density

- A point $x \in X$ is a **limit point** (or accumulation point) of $A$ if every open set $U$ containing $x$ contains at least one point of $A$ distinct from $x$.
- A subset $A \subseteq X$ is **dense** in $X$ if $\bar{A} = X$.  
  *Example*: $\mathbb{Q}$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}$ under standard topology.

## Practice Problems

1. Find the interior, closure, and boundary of $A = (0, 1] \cup \{2\} \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ with standard topology.
2. Prove that a set $C \subseteq X$ is closed if and only if $C = \bar{C}$.
3. Show that $\partial A = \partial (X \setminus A)$ for any subset $A \subseteq X$.
4. Prove that $\mathbb{Q}$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}$.
5. Give an example of a topological space containing a non-empty proper subset that is both open and closed (clopen).
