## Definition

Let $(X, \mathcal{T})$ be a topological space.

### Open Cover

A collection of open sets $\mathcal{U} = \{U_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in A}$ is an **open cover** of a subset $K \subseteq X$ if:

$$K \subseteq \bigcup_{\alpha \in A} U_\alpha$$

### Compactness

A subset $K \subseteq X$ is **compact** if every open cover of $K$ has a **finite subcover**. That is, whenever $K \subseteq \bigcup_{\alpha \in A} U_\alpha$, there exist infinitely many indices $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots, \alpha_n \in A$ such that:

$$K \subseteq U_{\alpha_1} \cup U_{\alpha_2} \cup \dots \cup U_{\alpha_n}$$

## Key Theorems

### Heine-Borel Theorem

> [!example] Heine-Borel Theorem (Euclidean Spaces)
> In $\mathbb{R}^n$ with the standard topology, a subset $K \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ is compact if and only if it is **closed** and **bounded**.

> [!warning] Fails in General Metric Spaces!
> In general metric spaces (e.g. infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces), closed and bounded sets are **NOT** necessarily compact.

### Extreme Value Theorem

> [!example] Extreme Value Theorem
> If $f : X \to \mathbb{R}$ is continuous and $X$ is compact, then $f$ attains a maximum and a minimum on $X$.

### Tychonoff's Theorem

The product of any family of compact topological spaces is compact in the product topology.

## Equivalent Characterizations

- **Finite Intersection Property (FIP)**: A space $X$ is compact if and only if every collection of closed sets with the finite intersection property has non-empty total intersection ($\bigcap C_\alpha \neq \varnothing$).
- **Sequential Compactness**: In metric spaces (but not all topological spaces), compactness is equivalent to sequential compactness (every sequence has a convergent subsequence).

---

## Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Assuming Closed + Bounded = Compact Everywhere
> Closed and bounded characterization holds **only** in finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces $\mathbb{R}^n$.

## Practice Problems

1. Show that the open interval $(0, 1) \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ is not compact by finding an open cover with no finite subcover.
2. Prove that every finite subset of a topological space is compact.
3. Show that a closed subset of a compact space is compact.
4. Prove that if $f : X \to Y$ is continuous and $X$ is compact, then the image $f(X) \subseteq Y$ is compact.
5. State the Finite Intersection Property characterization of compactness and prove it is equivalent to the open cover definition.
