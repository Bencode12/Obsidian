## Definition

A **topological space** is an ordered pair $(X, \mathcal{T})$, where $X$ is a set and $\mathcal{T} \subseteq \mathcal{P}(X)$ is a collection of subsets of $X$ (called **open sets**) satisfying the three axioms of topology:

| Axiom | Condition | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Empty & Whole Set** | $\varnothing \in \mathcal{T} \text{ and } X \in \mathcal{T}$ | Both the empty set and the entire space are open. |
| **Arbitrary Unions** | If $\{U_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in A} \subseteq \mathcal{T}$, then $\bigcup_{\alpha \in A} U_\alpha \in \mathcal{T}$ | The union of any family of open sets is open. |
| **Finite Intersections** | If $U_1, U_2, \dots, U_n \in \mathcal{T}$, then $\bigcap_{i=1}^n U_i \in \mathcal{T}$ | The intersection of any finite collection of open sets is open. |

## Canonical Examples of Topologies

> [!example] Standard Metric Topology
> For any metric space $(X, d)$, a set $U \subseteq X$ is open if for every $x \in U$, there exists $\epsilon > 0$ such that the open ball $B_\epsilon(x) = \{y \in X \mid d(x, y) < \epsilon\} \subseteq U$.

> [!example] Discrete Topology
> $\mathcal{T}_{\text{discrete}} = \mathcal{P}(X)$ (every subset of $X$ is open).  
> Induced by the discrete metric $d(x,y) = 1$ for $x \neq y$.

> [!example] Indiscrete (Trivial) Topology
> $\mathcal{T}_{\text{indiscrete}} = \{\varnothing, X\}$ (only the empty set and $X$ are open).

> [!example] Cofinite Topology
> $\mathcal{T}_{\text{cofinite}} = \{U \subseteq X \mid X \setminus U \text{ is finite}\} \cup \{\varnothing\}$.

## Bases and Subbases

### Basis for a Topology

A collection $\mathcal{B} \subseteq \mathcal{P}(X)$ is a **basis** for a topology $\mathcal{T}$ if:
1. Every $x \in X$ belongs to at least one $B \in \mathcal{B}$.
2. If $x \in B_1 \cap B_2$ for $B_1, B_2 \in \mathcal{B}$, there exists $B_3 \in \mathcal{B}$ such that $x \in B_3 \subseteq B_1 \cap B_2$.

The topology generated by $\mathcal{B}$ consists of all unions of elements in $\mathcal{B}$.

## Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Infinite Intersections
> Infinite intersections of open sets are **NOT** necessarily open!  
> *Example*: In $\mathbb{R}$ with standard topology, $\bigcap_{n=1}^\infty \left(-\frac{1}{n}, \frac{1}{n}\right) = \{0\}$, which is closed, not open.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify that the cofinite topology on any set $X$ satisfies the three axioms of a topology.
2. Let $X = \{a, b, c\}$. List all possible topologies on $X$.
3. Show that the collection of all open intervals $\mathcal{B} = \{(a, b) \mid a < b \in \mathbb{R}\}$ forms a basis for the standard topology on $\mathbb{R}$.
4. Prove that the intersection of two topologies $\mathcal{T}_1$ and $\mathcal{T}_2$ on $X$ is also a topology on $X$.
5. Is the union of two topologies $\mathcal{T}_1 \cup \mathcal{T}_2$ on $X$ always a topology on $X$? Prove or give a counterexample.
