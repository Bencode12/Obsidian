## Definition

A **ring** is a set $R$ equipped with two binary operations $+$ (addition) and $\cdot$ (multiplication) satisfying:

1. $(R, +)$ is an **abelian group** (identity $0$, inverse $-a$).
2. Multiplication is **associative**: $(ab)c = a(bc)$ for all $a, b, c \in R$.
3. Multiplication **distributes** over addition:
   - $a(b + c) = ab + ac$
   - $(a + b)c = ac + bc$

A ring is **commutative** if $ab = ba$ for all $a, b \in R$. A ring has a **unity** (or identity) if there exists $1 \in R$ such that $1 \cdot a = a \cdot 1 = a$ for all $a \in R$.

## Ideals and Quotient Rings

Let $R$ be a ring.

### Left, Right, and Two-Sided Ideals

A non-empty subset $I \subseteq R$ is a **two-sided ideal** (or simply **ideal**, written $I \triangleleft R$) if:
1. $(I, +)$ is a subgroup of $(R, +)$ ($\forall a, b \in I \implies a - b \in I$).
2. **Absorption under multiplication**: $\forall r \in R, \forall a \in I \implies ra \in I$ and $ar \in I$.

### Quotient Ring ($R/I$)

If $I \triangleleft R$, the additive quotient group $R/I = \{r + I \mid r \in R\}$ forms a ring under multiplication:

$$(r_1 + I)(r_2 + I) = r_1 r_2 + I$$

## Special Types of Ideals

Let $R$ be a commutative ring with identity $1 \neq 0$.

### Prime Ideal

An ideal $P \subsetneq R$ is **prime** if whenever $ab \in P$, then $a \in P$ or $b \in P$.

- *Theorem*: $P$ is a prime ideal $\iff R/P$ is an **integral domain**.

### Maximal Ideal

An ideal $M \subsetneq R$ is **maximal** if there are no ideals strictly between $M$ and $R$ (i.e. if $M \subseteq I \subseteq R$, then $I = M$ or $I = R$).

- *Theorem*: $M$ is a maximal ideal $\iff R/M$ is a **field**.

> [!info] Maximal Implies Prime
> In any commutative ring with $1$, every maximal ideal is prime ($M \text{ maximal} \implies M \text{ prime}$). The converse holds in PIDs.

---

## Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Confusing Subring and Ideal
> A subring must contain $1$ (or be closed under multiplication) and does NOT absorb multiplication from elements outside itself. An ideal absorbs multiplication from all elements in $R$!

## Practice Problems

1. Show that $n\mathbb{Z}$ is an ideal of $\mathbb{Z}$ for any $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.
2. Prove that $p\mathbb{Z}$ is a prime ideal of $\mathbb{Z}$ if and only if $p$ is a prime number.
3. Show that $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ is a field if and only if $p$ is prime.
4. Let $R = \mathbb{R}[x]$ and $I = \langle x^2 + 1 \rangle$. Show that $I$ is a maximal ideal in $R$ and that $R/I \cong \mathbb{C}$.
5. Give an example of a prime ideal that is NOT a maximal ideal.
