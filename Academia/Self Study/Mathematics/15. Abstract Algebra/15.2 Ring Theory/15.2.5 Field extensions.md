## Definition

Let $F$ be a field. A field $K$ is called a **field extension** of $F$ (written $K/F$ or $F \subseteq K$) if $F$ is a subfield of $K$.

The **degree** of the extension, denoted $[K : F]$, is the dimension of $K$ as a vector space over $F$.

- **Finite Extension**: $[K : F] < \infty$
- **Infinite Extension**: $[K : F] = \infty$

---

## Tower Rule (Degree Multiplication)

> [!example] Tower Rule
> If $F \subseteq L \subseteq K$ is a chain of field extensions, then:
> $$[K : F] = [K : L] \cdot [L : F]$$

---

## Algebraic vs Transcendental Elements

Let $K/F$ be a field extension and $\alpha \in K$.

- $\alpha$ is **algebraic** over $F$ if there exists a non-zero polynomial $p(x) \in F[x]$ such that $p(\alpha) = 0$.
- Otherwise, $\alpha$ is **transcendental** over $F$.

### Minimal Polynomial

If $\alpha$ is algebraic over $F$, there exists a unique monic irreducible polynomial $m_{\alpha, F}(x) \in F[x]$ of minimal degree such that $m_{\alpha, F}(\alpha) = 0$.

- *Degree Identity*: $[F(\alpha) : F] = \deg(m_{\alpha, F})$.

---

## Simple and Algebraic Extensions

- A simple extension $F(\alpha)$ is obtained by adjoining a single element $\alpha$ to $F$.
- An extension $K/F$ is **algebraic** if every element $\alpha \in K$ is algebraic over $F$.

> [!info] Finite Implies Algebraic
> Every finite extension $[K : F] < \infty$ is algebraic! The converse is false for infinite extensions (e.g. the algebraic closure $\overline{\mathbb{Q}} / \mathbb{Q}$).

## Practice Problems

1. Find the degree $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3}) : \mathbb{Q}]$ and write down a basis over $\mathbb{Q}$.
2. Find the minimal polynomial of $\alpha = \sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.
3. Prove that $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2}) : \mathbb{Q}] = 3$.
4. Show that $\pi$ is transcendental over $\mathbb{Q}$ and compute $[\mathbb{Q}(\pi) : \mathbb{Q}]$.
5. State the Tower Rule and use it to show that if $[K : F]$ is prime, then there are no intermediate fields between $F$ and $K$.
