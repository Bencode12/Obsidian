## Hierarchy of Domains

An **integral domain** is a commutative ring with $1 \neq 0$ having no zero divisors ($ab = 0 \implies a = 0 \text{ or } b = 0$).

| Domain Type | Definition | Key Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Field** | Every non-zero element has a multiplicative inverse. | $\mathbb{Q}, \mathbb{R}, \mathbb{C}, \mathbb{Z}_p$ |
| **Euclidean Domain (ED)** | Admits a norm $N : R \setminus \{0\} \to \mathbb{N}$ satisfying a division algorithm: $a = bq + r$ with $r=0$ or $N(r) < N(b)$. | $\mathbb{Z}, F[x], \mathbb{Z}[i]$ |
| **Principal Ideal Domain (PID)** | Every ideal $I \subseteq R$ is principal ($I = \langle a \rangle$ for some $a \in R$). | $\mathbb{Z}, F[x]$ |
| **Unique Factorization Domain (UFD)** | Every non-zero non-unit factors uniquely into irreducible elements. | $\mathbb{Z}, F[x], \mathbb{Z}[x], F[x, y]$ |

---

## Implications & Hierarchy Chain

> [!example] Strict Inclusion Chain
> $$\text{Fields} \subset \text{Euclidean Domains (ED)} \subset \text{Principal Ideal Domains (PID)} \subset \text{Unique Factorization Domains (UFD)} \subset \text{Integral Domains}$$

---

## Key Theorems

1. **ED $\implies$ PID**: Any Euclidean Domain is a Principal Ideal Domain.
2. **PID $\implies$ UFD**: Any Principal Ideal Domain is a Unique Factorization Domain.
3. **Gauss's Lemma**: If $R$ is a UFD, then the polynomial ring $R[x]$ is also a UFD.  
   *Consequence*: $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ and $F[x, y]$ are UFDs, but they are **NOT** PIDs (e.g. $\langle 2, x \rangle$ is not principal in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$).

## Practice Problems

1. Show that the ideal $I = \langle 2, x \rangle$ in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ is not a principal ideal, proving that $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ is a UFD but not a PID.
2. Prove that every field is a Euclidean Domain under the trivial norm $N(a) = 1$.
3. Show that in any PID, an ideal $\langle p \rangle$ is maximal if and only if $p$ is irreducible.
4. Verify that the Gaussian integers $\mathbb{Z}[i] = \{a + bi \mid a, b \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ form a Euclidean Domain with norm $N(a + bi) = a^2 + b^2$.
5. Give an example of an integral domain that is NOT a Unique Factorization Domain.
