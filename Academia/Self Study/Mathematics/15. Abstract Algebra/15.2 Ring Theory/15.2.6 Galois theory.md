## Definition

Let $K/F$ be a field extension. An **automorphism** of $K$ over $F$ is a field isomorphism $\sigma : K \to K$ such that $\sigma(a) = a$ for all $a \in F$.

The set of all such automorphisms forms a group under function composition, called the **automorphism group** $\text{Aut}(K/F)$.

### Galois Extension

A finite extension $K/F$ is a **Galois extension** if it is both **normal** (splits every irreducible polynomial that has a root in $K$) and **separable** (roots of irreducible polynomials are distinct).

When $K/F$ is Galois, its automorphism group is called the **Galois Group**:

$$\text{Gal}(K/F) = \text{Aut}(K/F)$$

- *Fundamental Identity*: $| \text{Gal}(K/F) | = [K : F]$.

## Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory

> [!example] Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory
> Let $K/F$ be a finite Galois extension with Galois group $G = \text{Gal}(K/F)$.
> 
> There is an inclusion-reversing **one-to-one correspondence** (bijection):
> 
> $$\{ \text{Intermediate fields } L \text{ such that } F \subseteq L \subseteq K \} \iff \{ \text{Subgroups } H \le G \}$$
> 
> given by:
> - $L \mapsto \text{Gal}(K/L)$
> - $H \mapsto K^H = \{x \in K \mid \sigma(x) = x \; \forall \sigma \in H\}$ (Fixed field)
> 
> Furthermore:
> 1. $[K : L] = | \text{Gal}(K/L) |$ and $[L : F] = [G : \text{Gal}(K/L)]$.
> 2. $L/F$ is a normal (Galois) extension $\iff \text{Gal}(K/L) \triangleleft G$. In this case, $\text{Gal}(L/F) \cong G / \text{Gal}(K/L)$.

## Unsolvability of the Quintic

> [!info] Abel-Ruffini Theorem
> A polynomial $f(x) \in F[x]$ is solvable by radicals if and only if its Galois group $\text{Gal}(K/F)$ is a **solvable group**.
> 
> Since the symmetric group $S_5$ is non-solvable, there exist quintic polynomials (degree 5) whose roots **cannot** be expressed using radicals ($+,-,\times,\div,\sqrt[n]{}$).

## Practice Problems

1. Compute the Galois group $\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3}) / \mathbb{Q})$ and list all its subgroups and intermediate fields.
2. Find the splitting field and Galois group of $x^3 - 2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.
3. Show that $\text{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$, generated by complex conjugation.
4. Explain why $x^5 - 6x + 3$ is not solvable by radicals over $\mathbb{Q}$.
5. State the Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory.
