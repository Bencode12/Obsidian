## Definition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring with identity $1$. The **polynomial ring** $R[x]$ is the set of all formal polynomials in $x$ with coefficients in $R$:

$$p(x) = a_n x^n + a_{n-1} x^{n-1} + \dots + a_1 x + a_0 \quad (a_i \in R)$$

The **degree** $\deg(p)$ is the highest integer $n$ such that $a_n \neq 0$.

---

## Division Algorithm for Polynomials over a Field

> [!example] Polynomial Division Algorithm
> Let $F$ be a field and let $f(x), g(x) \in F[x]$ with $g(x) \neq 0$. There exist **unique** polynomials $q(x), r(x) \in F[x]$ such that:
> $$f(x) = q(x)g(x) + r(x) \quad \text{where } r(x) = 0 \text{ or } \deg(r) < \deg(g)$$

## Irreducibility Criteria

A non-constant polynomial $p(x) \in F[x]$ is **irreducible** over $F$ if it cannot be factored into two polynomials of lower degree in $F[x]$.

### Low-Degree Test (Degrees 2 and 3)

A polynomial $f(x) \in F[x]$ of degree 2 or 3 is irreducible over $F$ if and only if it has **no roots** in $F$.

### Rational Root Theorem

If $f(x) = a_n x^n + \dots + a_0 \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ has a rational root $r/s \in \mathbb{Q}$ (in lowest terms), then $r \mid a_0$ and $s \mid a_n$.

### Eisenstein's Irreducibility Criterion

> [!info] Eisenstein's Criterion
> Let $f(x) = a_n x^n + a_{n-1} x^{n-1} + \dots + a_0 \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$. If there exists a prime $p$ such that:
> 1. $p \nmid a_n$
> 2. $p \mid a_i$ for all $0 \leq i \leq n-1$
> 3. $p^2 \nmid a_0$
> 
> Then $f(x)$ is **irreducible** over $\mathbb{Q}$.

## Practice Problems

1. Use the Polynomial Division Algorithm to divide $f(x) = x^3 - 3x + 5$ by $g(x) = x - 2$ in $\mathbb{R}[x]$.
2. Use Eisenstein's Criterion to prove that $f(x) = x^5 - 4x + 2$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$.
3. Show that $x^2 + 1$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{R}$ but reducible over $\mathbb{C}$.
4. Find all irreducible polynomials of degree 2 in $\mathbb{Z}_2[x]$.
5. State the Remainder Theorem and evaluate $f(3)$ for $f(x) = x^4 - 2x^2 + 7$.
