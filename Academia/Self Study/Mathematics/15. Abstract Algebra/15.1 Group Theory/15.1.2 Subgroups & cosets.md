## Definition

Let $(G, \cdot)$ be a group. A non-empty subset $H \subseteq G$ is a **subgroup** of $G$ (denoted $H \le G$) if $H$ itself forms a group under the restricted operation $\cdot$.

---

## Subgroup Criteria

To test whether a subset $H \subseteq G$ is a subgroup, we do not need to check all four group axioms.

### One-Step Subgroup Test

> [!info] One-Step Test
> A non-empty subset $H \subseteq G$ is a subgroup of $G$ if and only if:
> $$\forall a, b \in H \implies a b^{-1} \in H$$

### Two-Step Subgroup Test

> [!info] Two-Step Test
> A non-empty subset $H \subseteq G$ is a subgroup of $G$ if and only if:
> 1. **Closure under multiplication**: $\forall a, b \in H \implies ab \in H$
> 2. **Closure under inverses**: $\forall a \in H \implies a^{-1} \in H$

### Finite Subgroup Test

If $H$ is a **finite** non-empty subset of $G$, then $H \le G$ if and only if $H$ is closed under multiplication ($\forall a, b \in H \implies ab \in H$).

## Cosets

Let $H \le G$ and let $a \in G$.

| Concept | Definition | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Left Coset** | $aH = \{ah \mid h \in H\}$ | Shift of $H$ on the left by $a$. |
| **Right Coset** | $Ha = \{ha \mid h \in H\}$ | Shift of $H$ on the right by $a$. |
| **Index** | $[G : H]$ | The number of distinct left (or right) cosets of $H$ in $G$. |

> [!warning] Cosets are Not Always Subgroups
> A coset $aH$ is a subgroup of $G$ if and only if $a \in H$, in which case $aH = H$. Otherwise, $aH$ does not contain the identity $e$.

### Properties of Cosets

1. **Partition of $G$**: The set of left cosets of $H$ forms a partition of $G$. That is, every element of $G$ belongs to exactly one left coset.
2. **Equivalence Relation**: $aH = bH \iff a^{-1}b \in H \iff a \in bH$.
3. **Equal Cardinality**: Every coset $aH$ has the exact same number of elements as $H$ ($|aH| = |H|$).

---

## Lagrange's Theorem

> [!example] Lagrange's Theorem
> If $G$ is a finite group and $H \le G$, then the order of $H$ divides the order of $G$:
> $$|G| = [G : H] \cdot |H|$$

### Corollaries of Lagrange's Theorem

1. **Element Order Divides Group Order**: For any $a \in G$, $|a|$ divides $|G|$.
2. **Prime Order Groups are Cyclic**: If $|G| = p$ where $p$ is a prime number, then $G \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$ (and $G$ is generated by any non-identity element).
3. **Power Property**: For any $a \in G$, $a^{|G|} = e$.

---

## Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Assuming Converse of Lagrange's Theorem Holds
> If $d$ divides $|G|$, there is **NOT necessarily** a subgroup of order $d$.  
> *Counterexample*: The alternating group $A_4$ has order 12, but has no subgroup of order 6!

## Practice Problems

1. Use the One-Step Subgroup Test to show that $SL_2(\mathbb{R}) = \{A \in GL_2(\mathbb{R}) \mid \det(A) = 1\}$ is a subgroup of $GL_2(\mathbb{R})$.
2. Find all left cosets of $H = \{0, 3\}$ in the additive group $(\mathbb{Z}_6, +)$.
3. Let $G$ be a group of order 35. What are the possible orders of non-trivial proper subgroups of $G$?
4. Prove that if $G$ is a finite group of prime order $p$, then $G$ has no non-trivial proper subgroups.
5. Give an example of a group $G$, a subgroup $H \le G$, and an element $a \in G$ such that $aH \neq Ha$.
