## Definition

A **group action** of a group $G$ on a set $X$ is a map $\cdot : G \times X \to X$ satisfying:

| Axiom | Condition | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Identity** | $e \cdot x = x$ | The identity element acts trivially. |
| **Compatibility** | $(g_1 g_2) \cdot x = g_1 \cdot (g_2 \cdot x)$ | Group multiplication is compatible with action sequence. |

---

## Orbits and Stabilizers

Let $G$ act on $X$ and let $x \in X$.

### Orbit

The **orbit** of $x$ is the set of all targets $x$ can reach under the action of $G$:

$$\text{Orb}(x) = G \cdot x = \{g \cdot x \mid g \in G\} \subseteq X$$

- The orbits form a **partition** of the set $X$.

### Stabilizer

The **stabilizer** of $x$ is the set of group elements that leave $x$ fixed:

$$\text{Stab}(x) = G_x = \{g \in G \mid g \in G, \; g \cdot x = x\} \subseteq G$$

- *Theorem*: The stabilizer $G_x$ is always a **subgroup** of $G$.

---

## Orbit-Stabilizer Theorem

> [!example] Orbit-Stabilizer Theorem
> Let $G$ be a finite group acting on a set $X$. For any $x \in X$:
> $$|G \cdot x| = [G : G_x] = \frac{|G|}{|G_x|}$$
> That is, the size of the orbit of $x$ equals the index of its stabilizer subgroup in $G$.

---

## Burnside's Lemma (Cauchy-Frobenius Lemma)

Let $X^g = \{x \in X \mid g \cdot x = x\}$ denote the set of points in $X$ fixed by $g \in G$.

> [!info] Burnside's Lemma
> The number of distinct orbits $N$ of $X$ under the action of $G$ is equal to the average number of points fixed by elements of $G$:
> $$N = \frac{1}{|G|} \sum_{g \in G} |X^g|$$

---

## Applications

Group actions provide a powerful framework for:
- Counting symmetric combinatorial structures (coloring necklaces, graphs) using Burnside's Lemma.
- Proving class equations and Sylow Theorems by letting $G$ act on itself by conjugation ($g \cdot x = g x g^{-1}$).

---

## Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Confusing Orbit and Stabilizer
> - Orbit $G \cdot x \subseteq X$ (subset of the set $X$).
> - Stabilizer $G_x \subseteq G$ (subgroup of the group $G$).

## Practice Problems

1. Let $G = S_3$ act on $X = \{1, 2, 3\}$ by evaluation $\sigma \cdot i = \sigma(i)$. Find the orbit and stabilizer of $x = 1$.
2. State the Orbit-Stabilizer Theorem and use it to find the number of rotations that map a cube onto itself.
3. Apply Burnside's Lemma to count the number of distinct 4-bead necklaces that can be made using 2 colors (red and blue) under rotational symmetry $C_4$.
4. Let $G$ act on itself by conjugation ($g \cdot x = g x g^{-1}$). Describe the stabilizer $G_x$ (centralizer $C_G(x)$) and the orbit (conjugacy class).
5. Prove that the stabilizer $G_x$ is a subgroup of $G$ for any $x \in X$.
