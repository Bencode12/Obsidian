## Definition

A **group** is a set $G$ together with a binary operation $\cdot : G \times G \to G$ satisfying four fundamental axioms:

| Axiom | Mathematical Formulation | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Closure** | $\forall a, b \in G, \; a \cdot b \in G$ | The operation combines two elements to give another element in $G$. |
| **Associativity** | $\forall a, b, c \in G, \; (a \cdot b) \cdot c = a \cdot (b \cdot c)$ | Parenthesization does not affect the result. |
| **Identity Element** | $\exists e \in G \text{ s.t. } \forall a \in G, \; e \cdot a = a \cdot e = a$ | There exists a neutral element. |
| **Inverse Element** | $\forall a \in G, \exists a^{-1} \in G \text{ s.t. } a \cdot a^{-1} = a^{-1} \cdot a = e$ | Every element can be inverted. |

If $a \cdot b = b \cdot a$ for all $a, b \in G$, the group is called **abelian** (or commutative).

> [!info] Notation Conventions
> - **Multiplicative notation**: Operation written as $ab$, identity as $e$ or $1$, inverse as $a^{-1}$.
> - **Additive notation** (used primarily for abelian groups): Operation written as $a + b$, identity as $0$, inverse as $-a$.

## Key Concepts

### Order of a Group and Element

- **Order of a group** $|G|$: The cardinality of the set $G$.
- **Order of an element** $|a|$ or $\text{ord}(a)$: The smallest positive integer $n$ such that $a^n = e$. If no such $n$ exists, $a$ has infinite order.

> [!warning] Common Pitfall
> Do not confuse group order $|G|$ with element order $|a|$. While $|a|$ must divide $|G|$ for finite groups (by Lagrange's Theorem), individual elements can have order much smaller than $|G|$.

### Elementary Properties

1. **Uniqueness of Identity**: The identity element $e$ is unique.
2. **Uniqueness of Inverses**: For every $a \in G$, the inverse $a^{-1}$ is unique.
3. **Inverse of Product**: $(ab)^{-1} = b^{-1}a^{-1}$ (the "socks and shoes" property).
4. **Cancellation Laws**: If $ab = ac$, then $b = c$. If $ba = ca$, then $b = c$.

## Fundamental Examples

> [!example] Additive Groups of Numbers
> $\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Q}, \mathbb{R}, \mathbb{C}$ under addition $+$.
> - Identity: $0$
> - Inverse of $x$: $-x$
> - All of these are infinite abelian groups.

> [!example] Multiplicative Groups of Fields
> $\mathbb{Q}^* = \mathbb{Q} \setminus \{0\}$, $\mathbb{R}^* = \mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$, $\mathbb{C}^* = \mathbb{C} \setminus \{0\}$ under multiplication $\cdot$.
> - Identity: $1$
> - Inverse of $x$: $\frac{1}{x}$

> [!example] General Linear Group $GL_n(\mathbb{R})$
> The set of all $n \times n$ invertible matrices with real entries under matrix multiplication.
> - Identity: $I_n$
> - Inverse of $A$: $A^{-1}$
> - Non-abelian for $n \geq 2$ because matrix multiplication is non-commutative.

> [!example] Symmetric Group $S_n$
> The set of all bijections (permutations) $\sigma : \{1, 2, \dots, n\} \to \{1, 2, \dots, n\}$ under function composition.
> - Order: $|S_n| = n!$
> - Non-abelian for $n \geq 3$.

## Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Assuming Commutativity
> Unless explicitly stated that $G$ is abelian, $ab \neq ba$ in general. Therefore $(ab)^2 = abab \neq a^2b^2$ unless $G$ is abelian.

> [!warning] Forgetting Closure
> When checking whether a set and operation form a group, always verify that $a \cdot b$ remains inside the set $G$.

## Practice Problems

1. Prove that the identity element in any group $G$ is unique.
2. Show that if every element $x \in G$ satisfies $x^2 = e$, then $G$ must be abelian.
3. Find the order of every element in the cyclic group $\mathbb{Z}_6 = \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$ under addition modulo 6.
4. Let $G = GL_2(\mathbb{R})$. Find two matrices $A, B \in G$ such that $AB \neq BA$.
5. Show that the set of integers $\mathbb{Z}$ under multiplication does NOT form a group. Which group axiom fails?
