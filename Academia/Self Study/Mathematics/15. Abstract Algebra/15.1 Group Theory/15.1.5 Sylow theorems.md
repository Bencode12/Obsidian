## Definition

Let $G$ be a finite group and $p$ be a prime number dividing $|G|$. Write $|G| = p^k m$ where $p \nmid m$ and $k \geq 1$.

A subgroup $P \le G$ is called a **Sylow $p$-subgroup** of $G$ if $|P| = p^k$.

---

## The Three Sylow Theorems

The Sylow Theorems provide a partial converse to Lagrange's Theorem for prime powers and establish deep structural properties of finite groups.

### First Sylow Theorem (Existence)

> [!example] First Sylow Theorem
> For any prime $p$ dividing $|G|$, $G$ contains at least one Sylow $p$-subgroup $P$ of order $p^k$.

### Second Sylow Theorem (Conjugacy)

> [!example] Second Sylow Theorem
> Any two Sylow $p$-subgroups of $G$ are conjugate in $G$. That is, if $P_1$ and $P_2$ are Sylow $p$-subgroups, there exists $g \in G$ such that:
> $$P_2 = g P_1 g^{-1}$$
> Furthermore, any $p$-subgroup of $G$ is contained in some Sylow $p$-subgroup of $G$.

### Third Sylow Theorem (Counting)

> [!example] Third Sylow Theorem
> Let $n_p$ denote the number of distinct Sylow $p$-subgroups of $G$. Then:
> 1. $n_p \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$
> 2. $n_p$ divides $m = \frac{|G|}{p^k}$

---

## Uniqueness & Normality Criterion

> [!info] Crucial Classification Tool
> A Sylow $p$-subgroup $P$ is **normal** in $G$ ($P \triangleleft G$) if and only if $n_p = 1$.

This property is widely used to prove that certain finite groups are **not simple** (i.e., they possess non-trivial proper normal subgroups).

---

## Example Application: Groups of Order 15

> [!example] Proving Groups of Order 15 are Cyclic
> Let $|G| = 15 = 3 \cdot 5$.
> - For $p = 5$: $n_5 \equiv 1 \pmod{5}$ and $n_5 \mid 3 \implies n_5 = 1$. So $P_5 \triangleleft G$.
> - For $p = 3$: $n_3 \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$ and $n_3 \mid 5 \implies n_3 = 1$. So $P_3 \triangleleft G$.
> 
> Since $P_5 \cap P_3 = \{e\}$ and $P_5 P_3 = G$, $G \cong P_5 \times P_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}_5 \times \mathbb{Z}_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}_{15}$.  
> Thus every group of order 15 is cyclic!

## Practice Problems

1. Let $|G| = 200 = 2^3 \cdot 5^2$. What are the possible values of $n_5$? Show that $G$ cannot be simple.
2. Prove that every group of order 45 is abelian.
3. Find the Sylow 2-subgroups and Sylow 3-subgroups of the symmetric group $S_3$.
4. Show that no group of order 30 is simple.
5. Prove that if $n_p = 1$, then the unique Sylow $p$-subgroup is normal in $G$.
