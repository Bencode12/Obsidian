## Definition

In mathematical logic and model theory, a **first-order language** (or signature) $\mathcal{L}$ specifies the non-logical symbols used to build formulas:

1. **Function symbols**: $f, g, h, \dots$ each with a fixed arity $n \ge 1$.
2. **Relation symbols**: $P, Q, R, \dots$ each with a fixed arity $n \ge 1$.
3. **Constant symbols**: $c, d, e, \dots$ (functions of arity 0).

---

## First-Order Structure

A **structure** $\mathcal{M}$ for a language $\mathcal{L}$ (called an $\mathcal{L}$-structure) consists of:

- A non-empty set $M = |\mathcal{M}|$ called the **universe** (or domain) of $\mathcal{M}$.
- An **interpretation** for every symbol in $\mathcal{L}$:
  - Constant $c \in \mathcal{L} \implies c^\mathcal{M} \in M$.
  - $n$-ary function $f \in \mathcal{L} \implies f^\mathcal{M} : M^n \to M$.
  - $n$-ary relation $R \in \mathcal{L} \implies R^\mathcal{M} \subseteq M^n$.

---

## Satisfaction Relation ($\mathcal{M} \models \varphi$)

Let $\varphi(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ be an $\mathcal{L}$-formula and $a_1, \dots, a_n \in M$.

The relation $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi[a_1, \dots, a_n]$ means that the formula $\varphi$ is **true** in $\mathcal{M}$ under the assignment $x_i \mapsto a_i$.

> [!example] Model of Group Theory
> Let $\mathcal{L}_{\text{group}} = \{\cdot, e, ^{-1}\}$.
> The structure $\mathcal{M} = (\mathbb{Z}, +, 0, -)$ is an $\mathcal{L}_{\text{group}}$-structure that satisfies all group axioms:
> $$\mathcal{M} \models \forall x \forall y \forall z \; ((x \cdot y) \cdot z = x \cdot (y \cdot z))$$

---

## Elementary Substructures & Isomorphisms

- An **isomorphism** $\pi : \mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{N}$ is a bijection between universes preserving all functions, relations, and constants.
- $\mathcal{M}$ and $\mathcal{N}$ are **elementarily equivalent** ($\mathcal{M} \equiv \mathcal{N}$) if they satisfy the exact same first-order sentences ($\mathcal{M} \models \sigma \iff \mathcal{N} \models \sigma$).

## Practice Problems

1. Specify a first-order signature $\mathcal{L}_{\text{ring}}$ for ring theory and write down the sentence expressing commutativity of multiplication.
2. Let $\mathcal{M} = (\mathbb{N}, <)$ and $\mathcal{N} = (\mathbb{Z}, <)$. Are $\mathcal{M}$ and $\mathcal{N}$ isomorphic as poset structures? Are they elementarily equivalent?
3. Define what it means for a subset $A \subseteq M$ to be definable in an $\mathcal{L}$-structure $\mathcal{M}$.
4. Show that the set of even numbers is definable in $(\mathbb{Z}, +, \cdot, 0, 1)$.
5. Explain the difference between an isomorphism and elementary equivalence.
