## Syntactic vs Semantic Consequence

In mathematical logic:

- **Semantic Entailment** $T \models \varphi$: Every structure $\mathcal{M}$ that satisfies all sentences in $T$ also satisfies $\varphi$.
- **Syntactic Provability** $T \vdash \varphi$: There exists a formal proof of $\varphi$ from $T$ within a deductive proof system (e.g. natural deduction or Hilbert calculus).

---

## Gödel's Completeness Theorem

> [!example] Gödel's Completeness Theorem (1929)
> For any first-order theory $T$ and sentence $\varphi$:
> $$T \models \varphi \iff T \vdash \varphi$$

This fundamental result establishes the exact equivalence between **semantic truth** in all models and **syntactic provability** via symbol manipulation!

---

## Soundness and Satisfiability Formulations

### Soundness Theorem

$$T \vdash \varphi \implies T \models \varphi$$

If a sentence can be formally proved from $T$, it is semantically true in all models of $T$. (The proof system never proves falsehoods).

### Model Existence Form (Henkin)

> [!info] Model Existence Theorem
> A first-order theory $T$ is **consistent** (cannot prove a contradiction $T \nvdash \bot$) if and only if $T$ has a **model** ($\exists \mathcal{M} \models T$).

## Practice Problems

1. Distinguish between syntactic provability ($T \vdash \varphi$) and semantic entailment ($T \models \varphi$).
2. State the Model Existence formulation of Gödel's Completeness Theorem.
3. Why does Gödel's Completeness Theorem NOT contradict Gödel's Incompleteness Theorem?
4. Explain Henkin's technique of adding witness constants to construct a model from a consistent theory.
