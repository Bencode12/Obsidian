## Definition

A **deterministic Turing Machine** (TM) is a 7-tuple:

$$M = (Q, \Sigma, \Gamma, \delta, q_0, q_{\text{accept}}, q_{\text{reject}})$$

| Component | Description |
|---|---|
| $Q$ | Finite non-empty set of **states**. |
| $\Sigma$ | Finite **input alphabet** (not containing blank symbol $\sqcup$). |
| $\Gamma$ | Finite **tape alphabet** ($\Sigma \subset \Gamma$ and $\sqcup \in \Gamma$). |
| $\delta$ | **Transition function**: $\delta : Q \times \Gamma \to Q \times \Gamma \times \{L, R\}$. |
| $q_0 \in Q$ | **Start state**. |
| $q_{\text{accept}} \in Q$ | **Accepting state**. |
| $q_{\text{reject}} \in Q$ | **Rejecting state** ($q_{\text{reject}} \neq q_{\text{accept}}$). |

---

## Computation and Configurations

A **configuration** of a Turing machine represents its current state, tape contents, and head position: $u q v$ where $u, v \in \Gamma^*$ and $q \in Q$.

- **Accepting Configuration**: State is $q_{\text{accept}}$.
- **Rejecting Configuration**: State is $q_{\text{reject}}$.
- **Hanging / Infinite Loop**: Computation continues infinitely without reaching $q_{\text{accept}}$ or $q_{\text{reject}}$.

---

## Church-Turing Thesis

> [!example] Church-Turing Thesis
> The intuitive notion of an algorithm or "effective computation" corresponds **exactly** to functions computable by a Turing Machine.
