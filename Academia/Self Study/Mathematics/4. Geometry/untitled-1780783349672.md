# Geometric Transformations

## Overview

Transformations describe how points, figures, and coordinates change while preserving selected geometric properties.

| Transformation | Coordinate action | Preserved structure |
|---|---|---|
| Translation | $(x,y) \mapsto (x+a,y+b)$ | Lengths, angles, parallelism |
| Rotation | $(x,y) \mapsto R_\theta(x,y)$ | Lengths, angles, orientation |
| Reflection | Flip across a line | Lengths and angles, orientation reversed |
| Dilation | $(x,y) \mapsto (kx,ky)$ | Angles and shape, not usually length |

---

## Coordinate Representation

The rotation matrix about the origin is

$$R_\theta = \begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta & -\sin\theta\\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta\end{pmatrix}.$$

Compositions of transformations are represented by matrix products. The order matters: applying $A$ and then $B$ gives $BA\mathbf{x}$.

> [!warning] Common misconception
> A transformation can preserve shape without preserving size. Similarity transformations preserve angles and ratios, while isometries also preserve distances.

## Practice Problems

1. Find the image of $(2,-1)$ after a $90^\circ$ counterclockwise rotation.
2. Determine the transformation mapping $(x,y)$ to $(x+3,y-4)$.
3. Explain why the composition of two reflections can be a rotation.
4. Find the matrix for reflection across the $x$-axis.
