## Definition

Extremal graph theory studies the maximum or minimum number of edges a graph can have while satisfying certain structural constraints.

The **Turán Graph** $T(n, r)$ is the complete $r$-partite graph on $n$ vertices whose partition sets are as equal in size as possible (each set has size $\lfloor n/r \rfloor$ or $\lceil n/r \rceil$).

---

## Turán's Theorem

> [!example] Turán's Theorem (1941)
> Any graph $G$ on $n$ vertices that does NOT contain a complete graph $K_{r+1}$ as a subgraph has at most as many edges as the Turán graph $T(n, r)$:
> 
> $$e(G) \le e(T(n, r)) = \left(1 - \frac{1}{r}\right) \frac{n^2}{2}$$
> 
> Furthermore, $T(n, r)$ is the **unique** extremal graph attaining this maximum edge count.

---

## Mantels Theorem ($r = 2$)

> [!info] Mantel's Theorem (1907)
> The maximum number of edges in a triangle-free graph ($K_3$-free) on $n$ vertices is:
> $$e(G) \le \left\lfloor \frac{n^2}{4} \right\rfloor$$
> Attained uniquely by the complete bipartite graph $K_{\lfloor n/2 \rfloor, \lceil n/2 \rceil}$.

## Practice Problems

1. State Mantel's Theorem and find the maximum number of edges in a triangle-free graph with 10 vertices.
2. Construct the Turán graph $T(7, 3)$ and calculate its number of edges.
3. Prove Mantel's Theorem using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality or induction.
4. What is the maximum number of edges in a graph on $n$ vertices that contains no $K_4$?
5. Show that $T(n, r)$ is complete $r$-partite.
