## Definition

Let $X$ be a topological space. A **covering space** of $X$ is a pair $(\tilde{X}, p)$ where $\tilde{X}$ is a topological space and $p : \tilde{X} \to X$ is a continuous surjective map (called a **covering projection**) satisfying:

Every point $x \in X$ has an open neighborhood $U \subseteq X$ such that $p^{-1}(U)$ is a disjoint union of open sets $V_\alpha \subseteq \tilde{X}$:

$$p^{-1}(U) = \bigsqcup_{\alpha \in A} V_\alpha$$

and for each $\alpha \in A$, the restriction $p|_{V_\alpha} : V_\alpha \to U$ is a **homeomorphism**.

The sets $V_\alpha$ are called the **sheets** over $U$.

---

## Canonical Example: Real Line Covering the Circle

> [!example] Standard Covering $p : \mathbb{R} \to S^1$
> Consider $p(t) = e^{2\pi i t} = (\cos(2\pi t), \sin(2\pi t))$.
> 
> - The map wraps the real line $\mathbb{R}$ infinitely around the circle $S^1$.
> - The fiber over any point $z \in S^1$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}$.
> - This covering gives a rigorous proof that $\pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$!

## Lifting Theorems

Let $p : \tilde{X} \to X$ be a covering space with $p(\tilde{x}_0) = x_0$.

### Path Lifting Property

For any continuous path $\gamma : [0, 1] \to X$ starting at $x_0$ ($\gamma(0) = x_0$) and any choice of $\tilde{x}_0 \in p^{-1}(x_0)$, there exists a **unique** lifted path $\tilde{\gamma} : [0, 1] \to \tilde{X}$ such that:

$$p \circ \tilde{\gamma} = \gamma \quad \text{and} \quad \tilde{\gamma}(0) = \tilde{x}_0$$

### Homotopy Lifting Property

Any homotopy $H : Y \times [0, 1] \to X$ can be lifted uniquely to a homotopy $\tilde{H} : Y \times [0, 1] \to \tilde{X}$ given a compatible initial lifting.

## Universal Covering Space

A covering space $p : \tilde{X} \to X$ is called the **universal cover** of $X$ if $\tilde{X}$ is **simply connected** ($\pi_1(\tilde{X}) \cong \{0\}$).

> [!info] Universal Cover Theorem
> Every connected, locally simply connected topological space $X$ possesses a unique universal cover $\tilde{X}$ (up to isomorphism).  
> Furthermore, the deck transformation group $\text{Deck}(\tilde{X}/X)$ is isomorphic to the fundamental group $\pi_1(X)$.

## Practice Problems

1. Show that $p : \mathbb{R} \to S^1$ given by $p(t) = e^{2\pi i t}$ is a covering map.
2. Find the universal cover of the torus $T^2 = S^1 \times S^1$.
3. Show that $p : S^2 \to \mathbb{R}P^2$ (antipodal map) is a 2-fold covering map. What is $\pi_1(\mathbb{R}P^2)$?
4. State the Path Lifting Property and sketch its proof using open covers.
5. What is the deck transformation group of the covering $p : S^n \to \mathbb{R}P^n$ for $n \ge 2$?
