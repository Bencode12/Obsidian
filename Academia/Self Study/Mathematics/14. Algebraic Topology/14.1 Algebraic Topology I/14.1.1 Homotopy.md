## Definition

Let $X$ and $Y$ be topological spaces. Two continuous maps $f, g : X \to Y$ are **homotopic** (written $f \simeq g$) if there exists a continuous map $H : X \times [0, 1] \to Y$ (called a **homotopy**) such that:

$$H(x, 0) = f(x) \quad \text{and} \quad H(x, 1) = g(x) \quad \forall x \in X$$

We think of $H_t(x) = H(x, t)$ as a continuous deformation of $f$ into $g$ over time $t \in [0, 1]$.

---

## Homotopy Equivalence of Spaces

Two topological spaces $X$ and $Y$ are **homotopy equivalent** (written $X \simeq Y$) if there exist continuous maps $f : X \to Y$ and $g : Y \to X$ such that:

$$g \circ f \simeq \text{id}_X \quad \text{and} \quad f \circ g \simeq \text{id}_Y$$

- The maps $f$ and $g$ are called **homotopy equivalences**.
- Homotopy equivalence is a coarser relation than homeomorphism ($X \cong Y \implies X \simeq Y$).

## Contractible Spaces & Deformation Retracts

### Contractible Space

A topological space $X$ is **contractible** if it is homotopy equivalent to a single point space $\{*\}$.

> [!example] Examples of Contractible Spaces
> - Euclidean spaces $\mathbb{R}^n$ for any $n \ge 1$.
> - Convex sets in $\mathbb{R}^n$ (e.g. open/closed unit disk $D^n$).

### Deformation Retract

A subset $A \subseteq X$ is a **deformation retract** of $X$ if there is a homotopy $H : X \times [0, 1] \to X$ such that:
1. $H(x, 0) = x$ for all $x \in X$.
2. $H(x, 1) \in A$ for all $x \in X$.
3. $H(a, t) = a$ for all $a \in A$ and $t \in [0, 1]$.

> [!example] Circle as Deformation Retract of Punctured Plane
> The unit circle $S^1$ is a deformation retract of the punctured plane $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{(0,0)\}$ via $H(x, t) = (1 - t)x + t \frac{x}{\|x\|}$.

## Practice Problems

1. Show that homotopy of maps is an equivalence relation.
2. Prove that $\mathbb{R}^n$ is contractible.
3. Show that if $A$ is a deformation retract of $X$, then $A$ and $X$ are homotopy equivalent.
4. Prove that the cylinder $S^1 \times [0, 1]$ is homotopy equivalent to the circle $S^1$.
5. Are $S^1$ and $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{(0,0)\}$ homotopy equivalent? Explain.
