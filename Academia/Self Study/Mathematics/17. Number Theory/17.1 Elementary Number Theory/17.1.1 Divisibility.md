## Definition

Let $a, b \in \mathbb{Z}$ with $a \neq 0$. We say $a$ **divides** $b$ (written $a \mid b$) if there exists an integer $c \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that:

$$b = a \cdot c$$

If no such integer exists, we write $a \nmid b$.

---

## Division Algorithm

> [!info] Division Algorithm
> Given any integers $a, b \in \mathbb{Z}$ with $b > 0$, there exist **unique** integers $q$ (quotient) and $r$ (remainder) such that:
> $$a = bq + r \quad \text{where } 0 \leq r < b$$

---

## Greatest Common Divisor (GCD) & LCM

### Greatest Common Divisor

The **greatest common divisor** of two integers $a, b$ (not both zero), written $\gcd(a, b)$ or $(a, b)$, is the largest positive integer $d$ such that $d \mid a$ and $d \mid b$.

If $\gcd(a, b) = 1$, $a$ and $b$ are called **coprime** (or relatively prime).

### Least Common Multiple

The **least common multiple** $\text{lcm}(a, b)$ is the smallest positive integer $m$ such that $a \mid m$ and $b \mid m$.

- *Identity*: $\gcd(a, b) \cdot \text{lcm}(a, b) = |a \cdot b|$.

---

## Euclidean Algorithm & Bézout's Identity

### Euclidean Algorithm

The Euclidean Algorithm efficiently computes $\gcd(a, b)$ by repeatedly applying the Division Algorithm:

$$\gcd(a, b) = \gcd(b, a \bmod b)$$

### Bézout's Identity

> [!example] Bézout's Identity
> For any integers $a, b \in \mathbb{Z}$ (not both zero), there exist integers $x, y \in \mathbb{Z}$ (Bézout coefficients) such that:
> $$ax + by = \gcd(a, b)$$

## Practice Problems

1. Use the Euclidean Algorithm to find $\gcd(252, 198)$.
2. Find integers $x, y \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that $252x + 198y = \gcd(252, 198)$.
3. Prove that if $a \mid b$ and $a \mid c$, then $a \mid (bx + cy)$ for any integers $x, y \in \mathbb{Z}$.
4. Calculate $\text{lcm}(120, 45)$ using its relationship with $\gcd(120, 45)$.
5. Show that two consecutive integers $n$ and $n+1$ are always coprime.
